HUE-MAN

Color Vision Screening

Help us test Hue-Man

This is the same three-part screening built into the Hue-Man app: a plate test, a cap-sorting test, and a quick color-matching test. It takes about 5–7 minutes.

This web version exists so we can get feedback from a wider group of people. It's a screening prototype for our capstone project, not a medical device. It won't diagnose anything, but your results and feedback will directly help with the improvement of the app.

For the plate test, please view this on a normally lit screen (not in direct sunlight or a very dim room) and use your device's normal color settings (turn off Night Light / True Tone / blue-light filters if you can).

Part 2 of 3

Cap Sorting Test

You'll see 15 colored caps and one fixed reference cap. Drag each cap (or tap it) into place, one at a time, in whatever order makes the smoothest color gradient, starting from the reference. Each cap you place should blend into the one before it as closely as possible.

You can drag a placed cap to a different spot to reorder it, or drag it back into the available row to remove it. This part tests how finely you can tell apart small shifts in hue, not just whether you can name colors, so take your time; there's no timer.

Last part: Severity Test

Same choice the app gives you here: a quick fixed set of rounds, or a slower adaptive test that hunts down the exact color difference you can still tell apart on each axis.

Quick Mode: Odd-One-Out

15 rounds. Tap the one swatch that's a different color. About a minute.

Thorough Mode: Color Staircase

Adaptive: finds the smallest color difference you can reliably spot on each of three axes (red–green x2, blue–yellow). Takes longer, gives a more precise read than the quick test.

Instructions

Each plate hides a colored ring (a circle with a gap on one side) made of dots, sitting in a field of grey noise dots. Find the gap and tap the arrow that points the same way it does. If you can't tell, make your best guess. Difficulty adapts as you answer; it gets harder after two correct answers in a row, and eases off the moment you miss one.

Quick runs 15 rounds total (5 per axis). Standard runs 42 (14 per axis) and gives a steadier read. This is screening only, not a clinical diagnosis.
